Ruminance

It's Friday, September 25, 2026. Approximately noon. I've finished writing for the day. For the week, I reckon; my weeks lately run Saturday to Friday. The days sort of shift for me, in terms of beginnings and endings. Days aren't real anymore, nor are weeks or months. Just liminal stretches of time that waver between periods of practical usefulness and something akin to hibernation. I've been dormant for a while.

Sad news for a first blog of this kind, but I've archived approximately 20,000 words again because the vision for my novel has similarly shifted. Maybe saying the vision has shifted is wrong, after all I'm still writing about the same characters in the same setting and time period, but the characters have certainly shifted into unrecognizable versions of themselves. A lot of that has to do with the shift in eras. I could talk for ages about the settings I've been exploring. It's a bummer that so much research must be set aside, but frankly, whatever choice makes the story work is the right choice. I'm at least as confident in that as I am that my previous choices weren't working and were therefore the wrong choices to make.

Now according to the stat tracker in Novlr, I've written 2859 words over the last few days. My goal is 70,000 by January. I'm a little off pace, but there's some buffer built in. For one, I planned to take weekends off to focus on household chores, homework, meal prep. I've been distracted by a big project to try and degoogle my house (this is a tremendous effort, actually, despite the proliferation of open source alternatives). This meant dusting off my old networking and IT skills to repair and restore several old computers into functional server hubs. I swear, I won't dwell on this, but it's difficult to feel safe in my own home when I'm surrounded by technology that might be spying on me. I feel like an addict sometimes. Technology has a tight grip on me, so taking control of my home network and restricting what data leaves it has been a challenging but rewarding endeavor.

Back to the writing.

This time around, I've decided to trust my discovery process and lean into it, rather than trying to impose a rigid approach to drafting that is doomed to fail. I have an outline, and I know pretty certainly what is going to happen, who my characters are, and how they will change, but confining myself to filling out the steps between them until I hit a word count has been seriously detrimental to my creative process. So I'm experimenting. This week's experiment: at the end of each day's "discovery draft", I attach a bracketed "[]" footer that describes where I expect the scene, chapter, or draft to go from where I leave off. I'm eager to see how this pays off in the weeks and months to come and if it will at all.

This week, as I've already mentioned, I have begun again. This is my third revision so far, scoped down tremendously from a planned trilogy of revenge to a much more hollowed (and hallowed?) single novel told in first person. With this fresh start (granted, I have workable material that I suspect I can salvage from the now-archived revision 2), I have started a new prologue with the confession frame structure strictly in mind; I have written a new shakedown scene to replace a half-baked previous version; I have started writing a brand new scene where my protagonist goes on a date with the woman he expects to marry. I'm hoping these footers will act both as springboards for drafting new scenes and bridges for connecting them.

Aside from the work on my novel, I have done little to no writing. I have made a few Reddit comments, but that's about it. I have been brainstorming an idea for a game that I might actually be able to create on my own, but I have neither the time nor the energy to dedicate to it right now. I'm still wanting to learn more about writing interactive fiction using something like Twine, but I've found it too limiting and tricky to use for the more complex mechanics I'd like to use like persistent inventories and character sheets. I need to study this more.

Looking forward, I have a few goals in mind. First is to get full marks for my current class to further raise my GPA after last year's winter term, which was emotionally difficult. Next is to utilize the Tarot cards I purchased as the story generators they are, which I should probably leverage to write more poetry since I've been lacking in my "daily poem" exercise.

Now for a highlight from this week's discovery drafts: Metzgersohn was a bald, squat man, square in proportion and alarmingly vascular.

Until next week,

JD
